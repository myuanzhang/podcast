# Podcast script (Kimimi)

## Podcast Script
- **Title:** “CES 2026: The First Glimpse of Tomorrow”
- **Hosts:** Maya and Leo
- **Episode Summary:** Despite the show still being months away, early reports sketch out CES 2026’s focus on AI-everything, mobility labs, immersive entertainment, and the race to out-green previous tech cycles.

### Script  
**Maya:** Leo, tickets for CES 2026 just opened and the rumor mill is already spinning faster than a 240 Hz gaming monitor—what’s catching your eye first?  

**Leo:** Honestly, the headlines keep repeating one phrase: “AI won’t be a section, it’ll be the oxygen.” The Consumer Tech Association is telling exhibitors that every category—from fridges to fitness—needs to show on-device intelligence or risk looking dated.  

**Maya:** So we’re talking ChatGPT-style brains baked into hardware, not cloud-only tricks. Reports say Qualcomm and MediaTek both plan reference chips that let small appliances run large-language models offline, which could finally make privacy-first AI real.  

**Leo:** And if the chip guys deliver, car-makers are ready. Kia and BMW leaked that their 2026 concept dashboards will use those chips for “zero-latency voice mechanics.” Translation: you bark “find tacos near the charger,” and the car plots a taco-stop route before you finish chewing.  

**Maya:** Cars aren’t just getting smarter—they’re getting greener, too. Word is the show floor will have a “Mobility Test Track” where tire companies, plus Honda’s hydrogen spin-off, demo solid-state battery packs and swappable hydrogen capsules for delivery vans.  

**Leo:** Speaking of batteries, sustainability is turning into a badge of honor. CTA is requiring each booth to file a “carbon footprint sheet” for the first time. Big brands are leap-frogging each other with recycled-plastic casings and modular designs you can unscrew with one thumb.  

**Maya:** Recyclable hardware, nice—but what about recyclable content? The streaming wars are hitting a wall, so Sony and TikTok are co-sponsoring an “Immersive Storytelling Zone.” Rumor says they’ll unveil 8K micro-LED walls that shift aspect ratio to match whatever vertical, square, or wideshot clip you throw at them.  

**Leo:** That feeds straight into the crystal-ball prediction I keep seeing: foldable PCs finally growing up. Lenovo and Samsung filed booth plans detailing 18-inch tablets that fold down to 13-inch clamshells—no visible crease and supposedly under three pounds.  

**Maya:** Still, every year a miracle prototype dies in the supply chain. The chatter is that CTA will demand working units on the floor, not just pretty dummies, after last year’s rollable-phone fiasco.  

**Leo:** Good call. Meanwhile, the start-up pocket is where wild cards hide. Early applicants include a French company putting micro-cameras inside ski goggles to give real-time form coaching—imagine Apple Watch feedback, but projected on snow.  

**Maya:** And because Las Vegas summers now feel like surface-of-the-sun, HVAC tech is sneaking in. A Massachusetts start-up wants to demo a window-unit-sized heat-pump cube that uses CO₂ as refrigerant and claims minus-20 °C output without tearing up your energy bill.  

**Leo:** Put that next to the wave of countertop “induction grills” that hit 500 °C in ten seconds so renters can sear steaks without a gas line. Cooking gear is clearly courting TikTok chefs.  

**Maya:** Big picture: AI smarts, green credentials, and experience gimmicks. But the same reports warn of sticker shock—analysts estimate average component costs up 14 percent, meaning your next clever gadget could also be your priciest.  

**Leo:** So if you’re planning that January Vegas pilgrimage, maybe budget for the wow-factor tax too. We’ll keep tracking leaks; hit follow, and we’ll reconvene once the show floors open and the real games begin.  

**Maya:** Until then, stay curious—and maybe start training your voice for that taco-hunting car of the future.  

**Leo:** Thanks for listening, and we’ll catch you on the next tech ride!
